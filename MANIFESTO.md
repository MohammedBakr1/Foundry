# MANIFESTO

## Identity

The identity we've arrived at isn't tied to a specific domain name.

It is not:

> "A Systems Project"

It is not:

> "A Programming Course"

It is not:

> "A Project Factory"

It is:

> "A methodology for learning through understanding, building, experimentation, and contribution."

---

## The Core Sentence

> We start from Open Source, understand deeply, build to learn, and turn what we learn into software or contributions.

---

## Summary

Foundry doesn't want to tell the programmer:

> "What should you learn?"

Or:

> "What should you build?"

Instead, it helps them answer the more important questions themselves:

- What do I want to understand?
- What already exists?
- How does it work?
- Why does it work this way?
- What do I need to build to test my understanding?
- What did I learn from implementing it?
- How do I prove that I understood it?
- What will I do with what I learned?

From this, the cycle takes shape:

```
             OPEN SOURCE
                  ↓
               DEFINE
                  ↓
               EXPLORE
                  ↓
              UNDERSTAND
                  ↓
                BUILD
                  ↓
            INVESTIGATE
                  ↓
              VALIDATE
                  ↓
              REFLECT
                  ↓
          ┌───────┴────────┐
          ↓                ↓
       DEVELOP         CONTRIBUTE
          │                │
          └───────┬────────┘
                  ↓
             OPEN SOURCE
```

This is how Foundry becomes a real project with an applicable, testable, and evolving methodology — not just a name, a brand, or a set of ideas.

The ultimate goal is not for Foundry to produce as many projects as possible.

**The goal is to produce a programmer who understands what they're building, can build what they want to understand, and knows how to turn that understanding into something useful for themselves and for the community.**

---

## Foundry:

> Start from what exists.
> Understand before extending.
> Build when building teaches.
> Learn from implementation.
> Develop what has value.
> Contribute what can help others.
> Return knowledge to Open Source.
